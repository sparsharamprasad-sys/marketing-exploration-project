The brand & the founder

Meet Impulse and Sarah

Sarah Sarosh trained as an occupational therapist and began on YouTube as a medical student in 2017. Her honest beauty and lifestyle content grew into a community of more than 1.5 million people.

In 2021 she launched Impulse Coffees, inspired by her grandmother’s coffee. The press calls it India’s first creator-led instant coffee brand. It sells freeze-dried flavoured instant coffee with no chicory, plus iced teas, hot chocolate and sachets.

Origin & positioning

A gap in plain sight

Instant coffee in India meant two household names and a bitter reputation. Young people were curious about coffee, but a café cup at around ₹400 was out of reach for daily use.

Sarah spent a year on R&D and chose freeze-dried coffee for taste, aroma and kick, paired with USDA-certified organic flavours. Colourful labels and quirky names such as Nutkhat Hazelnut and Mocha-Pe-Chauka make the jar fun to own.

Founder as channel

Content that sells without selling

Her banner splits the channel into beauty, lifestyle, travel and business. Impulse appears inside that content the way it would in her own kitchen, which is why press calls the social content organic and relatable.

Her channel links straight to the store, so trust becomes a visit in one tap. By her own account, the first three years went into building her personal brand while Impulse ran largely on its own.

Co-creating

Tasting with the community

New flavours are tasted by the core team, then sent to top customers, who share tasting notes and record content. On Instagram, Sampling Saturdays have Sarah blind-tasting new flavours while customers guess.

Focus groups, Google forms and conversations feed the formula. The payoff: customers who wait for pay-day sales and save up, because they helped make the product.

Open process

Showing the messy middle

The hazelnut flavour took over a year. Samples from suppliers across India and even Switzerland were rejected until it tasted creamy and nutty enough. She called it Nutkhat Hazelnut, and the name stuck.

Sarah also talks openly about operations, including a warehouse fire that wiped out stock of the brand’s rechargeable frother, which is returning. Viewers see real setbacks and real fixes, and trust the label more for it.

Products

Flavour is the hero

Ten flavours lead the range, and Mocha-Pe-Chauka is the bestseller at 40,000–50,000 units. Hot chocolate is popular with mothers for its lower sugar, and iced teas attract people who rarely buy packaged iced drinks.

Iced teas come in blueberry, strawberry, lemon, pineapple and guava chilli. In February 2026 sachets launched, and the 30-sachet box sold out in pre-order. Fortified coffees with supplements are in development.

Growth

The growth story

Impulse has not published accounts, but the founder shared a picture. For its first three years revenue stayed around ₹1–1.2 crore a year with no significant marketing spend, repeat customers made up 60–65% of sales, and the business was stable and profitable.

The website is the strongest channel; Amazon and Blinkit (Mumbai) were added later. Mumbai is the biggest market, then Bengaluru, Delhi and Kolkata. Tracxn estimates revenue under ₹10 crore for the year to March 2025.

Audit verdict

What to keep, watch and do

Working

Founder storytelling, blind tastings, community feedback, an open process and a high repeat rate.

Watch

Flat ~₹1–1.2 Cr for three years. Reach sits with the founder (1.58M vs 39.5K on the brand page). Unit economics are unpublished, and supply hiccups can break momentum.

Next moves

1. A weekly behind-the-scenes series on the brand Instagram.
2. Publish tasting-panel results as numbers.
3. A creator and UGC programme.
4. Quick-commerce and offline distribution.
5. Channel-level ROI tracking.
