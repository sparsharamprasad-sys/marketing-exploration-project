Brewers to Viewers

ab.coffee · @abcoffeeindia · Instagram · October 2026
The question

Why every post has a job

The brand

ab.coffee sells single origin coffee, matcha and protein coffees through 90+ outlets and an app. Its Instagram does not read like a menu. It reads like a channel with five recurring shows.

The question

How does one coffee brand keep five different audiences watching, and what does each of them return to the brand?

The method

I read the public page, picked the five content types that keep coming back, and studied two examples of each: the format, the comments, and the call to action sitting in the frame. Every thumbnail in this document links to the original post.

Pillar one

Founder-centric

Funnel stage: Know

What it does

Gives the brand a face and a story. The founder (@brewwithabhijeet) speaks to camera about the build, with the claims carried by on-screen text: a stated goal of a ₹1000 Cr profitable coffee brand and 90+ stores already open.

Who it speaks to

Young, aspiring entrepreneurs who want to see how a brand gets built in India.

What we see on the page

  • Both reels are built on numbers and ambition: “Building a ₹1000 CR profitable coffee brand in India” and “90+ Stores”.
  • Comments read as admiration and curiosity: “Such an inspiring journey”, “Super proud of what you've built”. The founder replies and hearts comments himself.
  • Instagram offers the search prompt “ab.coffee founder journey” under the reel, so viewers are going looking for the story.

What the viewer takes away

A real route from first cup to 90+ stores, told by the person who built it. An aspiring founder gets a model to study and a reason to follow.

What the brand gets (the ROI)

Credibility at scale. A founder stating the numbers on camera carries more weight than an ad. Personal replies keep the comment section warm, and the format is cheap to shoot, so it keeps working long after it posts.

A founder on camera turns a coffee chain into a story.
Pillar two

Customer testimonies

Funnel stage: Trust

What it does

Builds trust through other people. Named customers are filmed outside real outlets with a cup in hand and answer in an interview style (Saakshi Bakliwal, Purva Juvekar).

Who it speaks to

Viewers who enjoy interview-style clips and food-suggestion content.

What we see on the page

  • Real storefronts stay in frame, with signs reading “Order on the app”, “Pickup for” and “100+ Beverages. Zero boring”.
  • Comments turn into menu tips: “The hazelnut coffee too!” and “Abcoffee hot chocolate”.
  • Viewers tag and cheer the speaker, so each testimonial reaches the speaker's own circle.

What the viewer takes away

Drink suggestions from people like them, and the confidence that a first cup is worth ordering.

What the brand gets (the ROI)

Peer proof lowers the risk of a first order. The outlet and the app prompt sit in every frame, so the testimonial also works as a call to action. The comment threads become a free list of menu favourites.

Strangers recommend it better than we can.
Pillar three

Trendy and trend-led

Funnel stage: Discover

What it does

Borrows attention that already exists. The Met Gala 2026 carousel pairs the event with the line “Couture in a cup, drama in every drop” over a Madonna “Vogue” edit. A barista reel puts a prompt on screen: “How many mistakes are you going to make today?”

Who it speaks to

People who follow trends, enjoy them and share them.

What we see on the page

  • The Met Gala graphic keeps ab.coffee's lime and a display serif, so a borrowed moment still looks like the brand.
  • The caption ties the event to the product (“Couture in a cup”), so the joke leads back to the cup.
  • At capture the carousel showed 6 comments and 5 reposts, the kind of signal that travels beyond followers.

What the viewer takes away

A reason to react, share and tag a friend. Fun first, coffee second.

What the brand gets (the ROI)

Reach beyond the follower base. Shares and reposts put the brand in front of people who don't follow yet, for the cost of a design and a caption. Staying in the trend conversation also keeps the page feeling current.

Pillar four

Sketches and short dramas

Funnel stage: Return

What it does

Turns the outlet into a set and the team into characters. One sketch plays out on the street outside a storefront. Another seats two uniformed staff on a bench with the on-screen line “or just buffering?”

Who it speaks to

Viewers who like short, episodic stories they can follow from one post to the next.

What we see on the page

  • Comments praise the acting (“Great acting”) and one calls out “epic eps” and “the whole cast”, which points to a series with a recurring cast.
  • The cast wear ab.coffee caps and uniforms in frame, and the storefront sign still reads “Order on the app”.
  • The performers comment back, so each episode is also shared by the people in it.

What the viewer takes away

Entertainment with characters worth coming back for, and a closer look at the people behind the counter.

What the brand gets (the ROI)

Repeat viewing. A recurring cast gives people a reason to follow for the next episode, and repeat engagement is what keeps a page in front of its own audience. The team becomes the face of the brand, which supports hiring and morale.

Staff as the cast, the outlet as the set.
Pillar five

Aesthetics

Funnel stage: Crave

What it does

Sets the visual tone and shows the product. The Infusions “Drop 7” post puts three drinks on a lime tile grid against an orange ground under a script headline, priced at ₹149 and marked “Available at select outlets”. The mango reel shows a layered shake being poured.

Who it speaks to

Viewers who are out for references and visual inspiration.

What we see on the page

  • Lime, orange and white tile, with script and sans type, repeat across posts, so the grid reads as one brand.
  • “Drop 7” labels a release in a numbered series, and the price and availability are in the post itself.
  • Instagram offers “mango thick shake recipe inspiration” under the reel, which shows the shot being read as a reference.

What the viewer takes away

Ideas to save, copy or order: a party mixer for the season, a shake to recreate at home.

What the brand gets (the ROI)

This pillar sits closest to the sale. Price, availability and the drop number give a viewer everything needed to walk into an outlet or open the app. Consistent visuals make a post recognisable before the name is read, which is brand recall without extra spend.

Pretty enough to save.
The takeaway

Five shows, one funnel

How the pillars work together

The founder gives people a reason to believe in the brand. Customers give them a reason to try it. Trends bring in people who have never heard of it. Sketches keep them coming back, and aesthetic posts make them want a specific drink. No single pillar carries the whole job, which is why the mix is the strength.

Why it justifies the spend

Every pillar ends at the same place: a cup, an outlet or the app. The feed covers awareness, trust, retention and purchase in one place, using the team, the customers and the outlets the brand already has. Content here is a growth channel and not decoration.
