The brief

Beauty and the Kapoor’s is a concept for a lip-first beauty brand fronted by Janhvi Kapoor. The name plays on a fairy tale, and the brand keeps that storybook spirit: a flower market at dawn, an Indian garden at golden hour, and a woman who treats getting ready as a small ceremony. This document sets out who the brand is for, what it looks like, how it launches and how it lives online, in enough detail for a team to start work on Monday.

The idea

Glam that looks like it took no effort and feels like a keepsake. Every product is a small luxury you can carry in a handbag.

The promise

Wearable colour with a polished finish, in shades made for Indian skin tones, at a price that feels like a treat rather than a stretch.

The first job

Turn Janhvi’s existing audience into a waitlist, the waitlist into first orders, and first orders into people who post their own swatches.

Status of this document. It is a working concept. The brand name and the founder’s involvement are to be confirmed by Janhvi’s team and by legal. Prices, targets and budget figures are proposed starting points based on assumptions, not market data, and should be tested against real costs.

Brand origin

Every brand needs a first moment, and this one has a picture. In a screenshot from her 9 October post, a lipstick is caught mid-swipe against a deep plum fur collar, a pink gloss held in the other hand, dark frames low on the nose, a warm wood-panelled room behind. The caption says the glam on this trip hit differently.

That is the origin story we tell: a travel-day ritual where the lip comes last and does the most. The brand grows out of a woman who collects small, beautiful things on the road and wants her make-up to feel like one of them.

Why the story works

  • It is rooted in something real that her audience has already seen and reacted to.
  • It puts the lip at the centre of the range, which sets the launch products.
  • It carries a place: rooms with lamplight, velvet chairs, fur and linen. That gives art direction a clear starting point.

The tone is warm and a little wry. She is in on the joke, and the brand invites the audience to be too.

Identity and logo

What changed

The earlier label-style logos are retired. This round follows your logo reference: a lowercase, high-contrast serif wordmark, a sharp change of weight inside the name, a four-point star and a small, widely spaced sans line beneath. Colour across the whole report now follows your pink and green palette. The logo ink is a deep crimson taken from the logo reference, so the mark stands out against the soft pastels.

How the logo is built

  • Weight contrast. “beauty” is light and “kapoor’s” is heavy, so the family name carries the brand and the fairy-tale “& the” sits between them in a small italic.
  • The star. A four-point star works like the glint on a lip gloss. Option A centres it above the name, and Option B lifts it over the stack in moss green. It can be used alone as a sticker, a loading icon or a lipstick-base stamp.
  • The spaced line. “Beauty house” in wide-set sans sits between “India” and “with love”, which roots the brand at home and keeps the tone warm.

Which to use. Option A is the primary logo for the website header, cartons and the letterhead. Option B suits square and tall formats, such as the profile photo, lipstick boxes and shopping bags. Keep clear space equal to the height of the star, and do not set the wordmark smaller than 30 mm wide in print or 140 px on screen.

Packaging and graphic language

Lipsticks come in lavender-blush cases with a crimson foil wordmark. The outer carton is cream card with a small star window, the tissue is azure, and the inside of each box is citron or moss green so opening it feels like walking into a garden. Every order includes a pressed-flower card with the shade story. Photography blends soft daylight in a flower meadow with warm lamplit rooms, using cosmos flowers, linen, velvet and fruit as props.

Made for India

Every choice in this plan starts from how Indian customers discover, test and buy beauty. These are the six facts that shape the range, the pricing and the channels.

Skin and shade

Indian skin spans fair to deep, with warm, neutral and olive undertones. The range and the online shade finder are built around undertone as well as depth and are tested on real faces from several regions.

Heat and humidity

Summer, monsoon and crowded commutes are everyday conditions. Lip products must stay put, not melt in a bag and not feather. We prove it with a wear test, not a claim.

How people shop

Discovery happens on Instagram Reels and YouTube, then many buyers move to a marketplace they trust. UPI, cash on delivery, a clear returns promise and delivery beyond the metros all matter.

Language and culture

Content runs in English and Hinglish, with creators in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi and more where the audience lives. Festivals and weddings are built into the calendar.

Price sense

Shoppers compare closely. We sit against mass-premium prices, with a mini lipstick and a gift set so the first purchase feels easy.

Trust

Plain ingredient lists, shared wear tests, quick answers on WhatsApp and honesty about what we can and cannot claim.

Audience and positioning

Positioning statement. For women who dress up for the small moments, Beauty and the Kapoor’s is the lip-led beauty house that makes everyday glam feel like a keepsake. It offers polished colour in shades made for Indian skin tones, at an accessible luxury price.

The Janhvi follower

Ages 18 to 28, follows fashion and film, saves looks on Instagram, buys her first premium lipstick for the packaging as much as the colour.

The ritual collector

Ages 25 to 35, working, travels for work or weddings, owns many lipsticks, wants fewer and better. Responds to gifting and refills.

The gift buyer

Buys for sisters and friends, needs a beautiful set under a clear budget, cares about presentation and easy delivery.

Shade and texture inspiration

Satin creams, glass-like gloss and a soft shimmer are the finishes to chase. The rose, coral, gold and olive swatches show the warmth we want for the blush, the gloss and any later highlighter.

Warm golds and deep roses flatter many Indian skin tones, so they guide the shade briefs for the manufacturer.

Product guidelines

  • Test every shade on a range of Indian skin tones, with warm, neutral and olive undertones, before locking the names.
  • Test wear in heat and humidity: no feathering, no melting in a bag, and a finish that lasts through a long day and a cup of chai.
  • Offer a mini lipstick at an entry price so first-time buyers can try the brand.
  • Choose a licensed contract manufacturer and confirm Indian cosmetics licensing and labelling rules with a regulatory adviser before any sale. Claims such as vegan or cruelty-free need proof.
  • Make claims only where testing supports them. Hydration and comfort claims need evidence.
  • Proposed prices: lipstick ₹899, gloss ₹749, liner ₹599, cream blush ₹999, mini lipstick ₹399. Gift set of lipstick and gloss: ₹1,499. Check these against manufacturing cost, taxes and competitor prices before they are final.

Digital presence

Digital is the shop, the magazine and the community all at once. Each channel has one job, and content is made once and cut for all of them.

Instagram

The home of the brand. Three posts and five story days a week. Formats: lip swatch reels, shade-story carousels, shade-of-the-week and customer reposts. Highlights: Shades, Ritual, Reviews, Orders. Captions in English and Hinglish, with Hindi and regional-language reels for tier-2 audiences.

YouTube

Slower films. Get ready with me, how a shade is made, a trip-in-a-makeup-bag. One film every two weeks, with Shorts cut from each.

Website

The flagship store. A shade finder by undertone, UPI, cards and cash on delivery, a pin-code delivery check, gift wrap, clear returns, and the waitlist from day one.

Community

A WhatsApp channel for drops and restocks, plus an email list with a monthly letter in the brand voice.

Creators

Makeup artists first, then 20 to 30 micro-creators with a mix of skin tones, cities and languages. Honest swatches matter more than reach.

Marketplaces

Nykaa, Myntra and Amazon India are the proposed next step after two to four weeks of direct sales, once reviews and stock are steady. Quick-commerce apps can follow in the metros.

Content pillars

  • The ritual: getting ready, the lip last.
  • The garden: flowers, shade stories, packaging close-ups.
  • The road: trips, rooms and places that inspire the shades.
  • The people: customer swatches, artists’ tips.

Story stickers

A set of illustrated props, such as a lipstick, brush, compact, claw clip and jade roller, becomes a free sticker pack for Instagram Stories. Followers use them in their own get-ready stories, which spreads the brand without any ad spend.

Recolour the props in the brand palette so every sticker feels like part of the same garden.

Launch-day run of show

Early morning: the hero film premieres on Instagram and YouTube. Midday: the creators post together and the website opens to the waitlist. Afternoon: live swatch session on Instagram. Evening: first customer reposts and a thank-you story revealing the pressed-flower card.

Paid media

Start small in the tease phase to grow the waitlist, then raise spend for the launch fortnight. Test three creative angles: the film, the swatch reel and the pressed-flower card. Retarget waitlist and site visitors first. Cut any angle that does not beat the average by week 12.

Press and partnerships

Send a hand-labelled box to beauty editors and stylists. Pitch the origin story and the keepsake idea. Look for a design collaboration with an Indian stationery or craft label for the second drop.

Risks and how we handle them

  • Name and trademark conflict. Search first and register early.
  • Shade mismatch. Test widely and keep the shade finder honest. Offer swaps.
  • Stock-outs or delays. Order for a modest first run and keep the waitlist informed.
  • Claims and regulation. Every claim and label is approved by a regulatory adviser.
  • Festive stock pressure. Order extra of the hero shades before Diwali and confirm delivery partners’ cut-off dates.
  • Founder dependence. Build the world around the brand, not only the face.
